Inflation in Portugal hits 3.8% in October 2026: what it means for you

Updated 2026-11-12 · 6 min read

Inflation in Portugal reached 3.8% in October 2026, its highest level since May 2023, according to final figures from the national statistics office (INE) released on 11 November. Energy prices did most of the pushing. Here is what that means for savings and variable-rate mortgages, explained with simple examples.

Inflation in Portugal climbed to 3.8% in October 2026, the national statistics office INE confirmed on Wednesday, 11 November. That is 0.2 percentage points above September (3.6%) and the highest annual rate since May 2023. Prices have now accelerated for three months in a row, and energy is the main reason: energy products cost 17.4% more than a year earlier.

Below we look at what the data actually shows, why prices rose, what a 3.8% inflation rate means if you hold cash or have a variable-rate mortgage, and what to keep in mind before changing anything.

The final data confirmed the flash estimate published on 30 October. The table sums up the key indicators.

The CPI is Portugal's main national measure. The HICP (Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices) follows common EU rules and is the one used to compare countries. Portugal's HICP came in at 3.9%, slightly below Eurostat's estimate for the euro area average, according to the Portuguese business outlet ECO.

Why did inflation rise in October?

In short: fuel and energy. Annual energy inflation went from 15.3% in September to 17.4% in October. Throughout the year, energy markets have been under pressure from the conflict in the Middle East that began in February, which pushed oil and gas prices higher.

Unprocessed food (fresh fruit, vegetables, meat and fish) held steady at 3.7%. And the monthly change was only 0.2%, far below September's 1.2%. In other words, prices rose only slightly from September to October. The annual rate went up mainly because prices had been flat in October 2025, a so-called base effect.

What is core inflation and why does it matter?

Core inflation strips out energy and unprocessed food, the most volatile items. It helps show whether price rises are spreading to the rest of the economy.

In October, core inflation edged up from 2.7% to 2.8%. That is a small move, suggesting the pressure is still concentrated in energy. It is worth watching, though: when energy stays expensive for a long time, it tends to feed into transport, services and other goods.

What does 3.8% inflation mean for your savings?

A 3.8% inflation rate means that, on average, something that cost 100 euros a year ago now costs about 103.80 euros. If your money sits idle, you are quietly losing purchasing power.

A hypothetical example with round numbers: say you keep 10,000 euros in a current account paying no interest. If inflation stayed at 3.8% for the next 12 months, in a year that money would buy roughly what 9,634 euros buys today. The balance is the same, but its real value has shrunk by about 366 euros.

How much does a savings account need to pay to beat inflation?

In Portugal, interest on deposits is generally taxed at 28%. What matters is the net rate after tax, compared with inflation. The hypothetical table below assumes inflation stays at 3.8%:

Gross rate (hypothetical)Net rate (after 28% tax)Approximate real return
0%0%-3.7%
2%1.44%-2.3%

In this scenario, a deposit would need to pay around 5.3% gross just to break even. That does not make deposits useless: they remain the right place for an emergency fund, because they are safe and easy to access. But it shows why cash left idle for years tends to lose value when inflation is high.

Keep in mind that nobody knows what inflation will be over the next 12 months. It could ease if energy prices fall, or stay high. The example is purely illustrative.

Inflation in Portugal and your mortgage payment

Inflation shapes the decisions of the ECB (European Central Bank), which targets 2% inflation over the medium term. After raising rates by 25 basis points in September, the ECB left them unchanged on 29 October, with the deposit facility rate at 2.50%.

At the end of October, money markets saw another hike in December as likely, according to the Portuguese daily Diário de Notícias. That is a market expectation, not a decision: the ECB said it is not pre-committing to any particular rate path.

Expectations of higher rates show up in Euribor, the benchmark for most variable-rate mortgages in Portugal. The 12-month Euribor stood at 3.248% on 6 November, according to the Lusa news agency. In a simulation by consumer group Deco Proteste reported by Lusa, a 150,000-euro, 30-year loan with a 1% spread, linked to 12-month Euribor and reset in November, now costs 736.94 euros a month, 89.31 euros more than a year ago.

Every contract is different. The payment depends on the Euribor tenor, the spread, the outstanding balance and the reset date, so it is best to check with your bank.

How can you protect your savings from inflation?

There is no risk-free answer. But a few principles help you decide calmly:

  • Keep an emergency fund in safe, easy-access products, even if they earn less than inflation. The job of that money is to be available, not to grow.
  • Compare rates and terms on deposits and government savings products. Conditions change when interest rates rise.
  • Think long term for money you will not need soon: shares, bonds and other assets can keep pace with or beat inflation over many years, but they can fall sharply in the short run.
  • Diversify across asset types so you are not relying on a single outcome.
  • Invest gradually with regular amounts, rather than making one big move because of a single monthly figure.
  • Factor in costs and taxes, which reduce the real return of any investment.

Risks to keep in mind

Trying to beat inflation should never mean taking risks you do not understand:

  • Assets with higher return potential can lose value, sometimes heavily and for long periods.
  • Existing bonds tend to fall in price when interest rates rise.
  • Gold and other so-called safe havens also move up and down and pay no interest.
  • One month of high inflation does not set a trend. Impulsive decisions are often costly.

The bottom line

Inflation in Portugal rose to 3.8% in October 2026, driven by energy, while core inflation remains more contained at 2.8%. For households, it shows up in shop prices, in the purchasing power of savings and, for those on variable rates, in mortgage payments. The next key date is the ECB's December meeting.
