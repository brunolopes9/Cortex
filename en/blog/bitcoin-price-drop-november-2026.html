Bitcoin price drop: why it fell to $64,000 in November 2026

Updated 2026-11-25 · 6 min read

Bitcoin traded at around $64,000 on 16 November, after touching roughly $62,000 over the weekend, its lowest level since July. The bitcoin price drop was driven by ETF outflows, fears of higher US interest rates and forced selling of leveraged positions.

The bitcoin price drop in November 2026 pushed the world's largest cryptocurrency into the $62,000 to $64,000 range. According to DECO PROteste Investe, a Portuguese consumer investment publication, bitcoin hit $61,946 over the weekend of 14–15 November, its lowest price since 13 July. On the morning of 16 November it was trading close to $64,000, as reported by Fortune and Exame.

Below we look at what happened, why it happened and what it may mean for anyone who holds bitcoin or is thinking about it. No forecasts, no panic.

Bitcoin price drop: what happened in November

Over the autumn, bitcoin had climbed back above $80,000. In November that rally was largely wiped out. The price first lost the $70,000 level, then $67,000, and finally tested $62,000.

Several sources put the fall since the start of the month at more than 15%. Compared with its all-time high of around $126,000 in October 2025, bitcoin is now worth roughly half.

Why is bitcoin falling?

There is no single cause. The sources we checked point to several factors that fed into each other.

Money leaving bitcoin ETFs

US spot bitcoin ETFs saw redemptions for several days in a row in early November. DECO PROteste Investe counts about $1.44 billion of outflows between 5 and 11 November. When a fund has to pay investors back, it sells bitcoin, which weighs on the price.

Fears of higher US interest rates

Markets have started to price in a tougher stance from the Federal Reserve, the US central bank, against a backdrop of sticky inflation. US Treasury yields have risen. When you can earn more on low-risk bonds, a volatile asset with no income, such as bitcoin, becomes less appealing to some investors.

Leverage and forced selling

Many traders buy bitcoin with borrowed money. When the price falls, platforms automatically close those positions once margin runs out. That automatic selling pushes the price lower, which triggers more forced selling, in a chain reaction.

Strategy and the MSCI indices

MSCI decided to remove Strategy, the listed company that holds one of the largest bitcoin treasuries, from its indices from 30 November. Funds tracking those indices will have to sell the shares, and the market read the decision as another negative signal for the sector.

Lower appetite for risk

Stock markets were also under pressure, especially technology shares. When investors turn cautious, bitcoin has tended to behave more like a speculative asset than a safe haven.

What about Europe? The interest rate backdrop

On this side of the Atlantic, the European Central Bank (ECB) kept its deposit rate at 2.50% at its 29 October meeting, after two hikes in 2026. A number of analysts expect another increase in December. Higher rates in Europe also make savings accounts and bonds look relatively better next to assets that pay no income.

There is also a currency effect. Bitcoin is priced in dollars, so for a euro-based investor the result also depends on how the euro moves against the dollar.

How much does someone who bought higher lose? A simple example

Take a purely hypothetical example with round numbers. Anna invested €1,000 when bitcoin was worth $100,000. Ignoring exchange rates and fees, at $64,000 her investment would be worth about €640, a 36% loss.

The part people often miss: to get back to €1,000, bitcoin does not need to rise 36%. It needs to rise about 56%. The deeper the fall, the bigger the gain needed to recover.

This is why drawdown, the fall from the highest point, is one of the most useful risk measures for any investment.

After the bitcoin price drop: sell, buy or wait?

Nobody knows whether bitcoin has found a floor. Some asset managers see current prices as a long-term opportunity, while some analysts do not rule out a move below $60,000. Both are opinions, not certainties.

Rather than trying to guess the next move, it helps to answer a few questions:

  • How much of my portfolio is in bitcoin? DECO PROteste Investe, for example, suggests that people who invest in crypto keep it below 5% of their overall portfolio.
  • Am I using leverage? With borrowed money, a fall like this can wipe out the capital invested.
  • Will I need this money in the next few years? Emergency savings or money for near-term goals should not sit in such a volatile asset.
  • Did I have a plan before buying? Decisions made in the heat of panic or euphoria rarely end well.

Risks to keep in mind

  • High volatility: falls of 20% or more within a few weeks have happened many times in bitcoin's history.
  • No income of its own: bitcoin pays no interest or dividends; returns depend entirely on price.
  • Currency risk: for euro investors, the dollar matters too.
  • Platform risk: know where your assets are held and what protection applies.
  • Leverage: it magnifies both gains and losses and can lead to positions being closed automatically.
