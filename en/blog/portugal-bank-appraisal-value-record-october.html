Bank appraisal value in Portugal hits a record €2,282 per m² in October

Updated 2026-11-28 · 6 min read

The median bank appraisal value in Portugal rose to €2,282 per square metre in October 2026, a new record, according to the national statistics office INE. Values were 12.7% higher than a year earlier, a slightly slower pace than in September. Here is what that means if you are planning to take out a mortgage in Portugal.

Bank appraisal value in Portugal: what INE reported

The bank appraisal value in Portugal climbed again in October 2026, reaching a median of €2,282 per square metre. That is the highest level on record, according to data published by INE (Statistics Portugal) on 25 November.

Compared with September, the median went up by €4, or roughly 0.2%. Compared with October 2025, it was 12.7% higher, down from an annual rise of 14.2% the month before.

In short, appraisals are still rising, but the pace has eased a little.

INE's figures are based on 31,394 appraisals carried out during the month (19,372 apartments and 12,022 houses). That is 7.4% fewer than a year earlier and 3.7% fewer than in September.

What is a bank appraisal?

When you apply for a mortgage in Portugal, the bank does not simply lend against the price you agreed with the seller. Before approving the loan, it sends a qualified valuer to inspect the property and estimate what it is worth.

INE collects these appraisals every month and calculates the median value per square metre. The median is the middle figure: half of the properties were valued above it and half below.

Two things are worth keeping in mind:

  • It is not the sale price. It is the valuer's estimate on behalf of the bank, and it can come in above or below the agreed price.
  • It only covers mortgage-backed purchases. Cash purchases are not included in these figures.

Even so, it is a useful gauge of the market, because it is published monthly and shows the value banks are willing to accept as collateral.

Why does the appraisal matter so much for a mortgage?

For a main residence, the general rule set by Banco de Portugal is that the loan should not exceed 90% of the lower of two figures: the purchase price or the bank appraisal value.

So if the appraisal comes in below the price, you have to cover the gap yourself, on top of the usual deposit.

A simple example (hypothetical figures)

Imagine a property with an agreed price of €250,000.

ScenarioAppraisalMaximum loan (90%)Deposit needed
A: appraisal matches the price€250,000€225,000€25,000
B: appraisal below the price€240,000€216,000€34,000

In scenario B, an appraisal €10,000 below the price means finding an extra €9,000 of your own money. Transfer tax, stamp duty and notary costs come on top and are not included here.

That is why rising appraisals make it more likely that the valuation will match the asking price. The flip side is that homes are more expensive and loans are larger.

What would a home cost at the median value?

A rough guide is to multiply the floor area by the value per square metre. Using round numbers, purely as an illustration:

  • An 80 m² apartment at the national apartment median (€2,670/m²) would come to about €214,000.
  • The same apartment at the Greater Lisbon figure (€3,548/m²) would come to about €284,000.
  • A 150 m² house at the national house median (€1,640/m²) would come to about €246,000.

In reality every property is valued individually. Location, condition, floor, sun exposure and energy rating all affect the result.

Where are appraisal values highest?

Greater Lisbon, the Algarve and the Setúbal Peninsula remain the most expensive regions, at 52.3%, 30.4% and 24.7% above the national median respectively.

For apartments, the lowest values were in the Alentejo (€1,713/m²) and the Centre region (€1,740/m²). For houses, the lowest were in the Centre (€1,185/m²) and the Alentejo (€1,274/m²).

The strongest annual increase was in the Oeste and Vale do Tejo region (16.4%), and no region recorded a fall compared with October 2025. Month on month, the Azores were the only region to decline (-0.7%).

Is the slowdown a sign of change?

The annual rise slowed from 14.2% in September to 12.7% in October. That is a slowdown, but a single month is not enough to call a turn in the trend.

The number of appraisals also fell 7.4% year on year. Since each appraisal is linked to a mortgage application, this may point to fewer applications reaching that stage. INE does not give reasons, though, so it is best not to jump to conclusions.

What about interest rates and the ECB?

At its last meeting, on 29 October, the European Central Bank (ECB) kept its deposit rate at 2.5%. President Christine Lagarde said the bank was well placed to wait for more data before acting. The next monetary policy meeting is scheduled for December.

This matters for variable-rate borrowers because Euribor, the benchmark for most Portuguese mortgages, tends to follow expectations about ECB rates. With pricier homes and bigger loans, monthly payments become more sensitive to every move in Euribor.

What are the risks for buyers right now?

  • Low appraisal: you may need more cash, as the example above shows.
  • Variable rates: a rise in Euribor increases your monthly payment.
  • Prices do not rise forever: appraisals can level off or fall, and a home bought at a peak may take years to regain its value.
  • Affordability: a larger loan ties up a bigger share of your income for many years.

What can you do before applying for a mortgage?

  1. Run the numbers with an interest rate higher than today's to test how much room your budget has.
  2. Keep a buffer in case the appraisal comes in below the price.
  3. Compare offers from several banks, looking at the APR (TAEG in Portugal) and at appraisal and insurance costs.
  4. Include taxes and notary fees when planning your deposit.
  5. Keep an emergency fund separate from your deposit money.

Frequently asked questions

What is a bank appraisal value in Portugal?

It is the estimated value of a property made by a valuer hired by the bank when someone applies for a mortgage. INE publishes the median value per square metre of these appraisals every month.

What was the bank appraisal value per m² in October 2026?

According to INE, the median was €2,282 per square metre for all housing, €2,670 for apartments and €1,640 for houses.

What happens if the appraisal is lower than the purchase price?

The bank bases the loan on the lower of the price and the appraisal. The buyer has to cover the difference with their own money, on top of the deposit.

How much will a Portuguese bank lend to buy a home?

For a main residence, the general rule from Banco de Portugal is a maximum of 90% of the lower of the purchase price and the appraisal. The final amount also depends on your income, debt-to-income ratio and profile.

Does a record appraisal mean prices will keep rising?

Not necessarily. The data shows what happened up to October. Appraisals can slow, stabilise or fall in the future, and the annual increase was already smaller in October than in September.
