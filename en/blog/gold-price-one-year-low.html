Gold price falls to a one-year low: what's driving the drop

Updated 2026-11-26 · 6 min read

The gold price posted its biggest weekly fall since March, touching around $3,514 an ounce on Friday 20 November, its lowest level since September 2025. The main driver is growing expectation of further US interest rate rises, which lifts the dollar and makes a non-yielding asset like gold less appealing.

The gold price has just had its worst week since March. On Friday 20 November 2026, spot gold dipped to roughly $3,514 an ounce, its lowest point since early September 2025, according to Reuters. Bloomberg reported five consecutive losing sessions. Put simply, the metal many savers treat as a safe haven is now around a third below the record it set at the end of January.

Below we look at what happened, why it happened, what it means if you save or invest in euros, and which risks are worth keeping in mind before making any decision.

What happened to the gold price this week?

The slide began early in the week and picked up speed on Friday. Financial news services put the cumulative weekly fall at roughly 8% to 9%. The exact figure varies slightly because some sources quote the spot price and others quote futures contracts.

The key numbers help put the move in context:

Silver and other metals also fell sharply over the same week, so this was not a gold-only story.

Why is gold falling?

There is no single cause, but leading financial sources point to three factors that feed into one another.

The US Federal Reserve is signalling more rate rises

Gold pays no interest and no dividends. When rates go up, cash deposits and bonds start paying more, and gold loses some of its appeal by comparison. This week, signals from the US Federal Reserve strengthened the view that more increases are coming, and markets moved to price a December hike as highly likely.

A stronger dollar

Gold is priced in dollars. When the dollar strengthens, the metal becomes more expensive for buyers using other currencies, which tends to dampen demand. Rising US Treasury yields added to the pressure.

Forced selling after the AI stock sell-off

Bloomberg linked part of the pressure to the rout in shares tied to artificial intelligence. When equity portfolios take heavy losses, some investors sell assets that are still showing gains, such as gold bought earlier, to raise cash. It is a familiar pattern in nervous markets and often has little to do with anyone's long-term view on the metal.

What does the falling gold price mean for European investors?

In euros, the drop looks slightly different

Headline prices are quoted in dollars, but if you invest from the euro area, what matters is the value in euros. Because the dollar has strengthened, the fall measured in euros tends to be a little smaller than the fall in dollars. That is not a guaranteed cushion, but it matters when you compare today's value with the price you paid.

What about the ECB (European Central Bank)?

At its last meeting on 29 October, the ECB (European Central Bank) kept its deposit rate at 2.5%. The next decision is due on 16 and 17 December, and several analysts expect another rise, although that remains a forecast, not a fact. Higher euro area rates tend to work the same way as in the US: they make deposits and bonds relatively more attractive than gold.

Physical gold, ETFs and CFDs

The impact depends on how you hold gold. If you own bars or coins, you have a paper loss, which only becomes real if you sell. If you hold a gold ETF, the fall shows up in the value of your units. If you trade leveraged products such as CFDs, your losses can be much larger than the move in gold itself, because leverage magnifies price changes in both directions.

A simple example: how much a gold fall weighs on a portfolio

Take a purely hypothetical portfolio of €20,000 with 10% in gold, or €2,000. If gold drops 30%, that slice is worth €1,400. The loss is €600, which is 3% of the whole portfolio.

Now picture someone who put the full €20,000 into gold. The same 30% fall would leave them with €14,000, a loss of €6,000. The asset is identical; what changes is how much of the total it represents. That is why diversification and deciding in advance how much you can afford to lose matter so much.

Is it worth buying gold now that it is cheaper?

There is no single right answer. A price below the record does not mean gold is cheap, nor that it will bounce back soon. Gold has gone through long stretches of decline or sideways movement before.

A few questions worth asking first:

  • What is the goal? Long-term diversification is very different from trying to profit from a quick rebound.
  • What share of the portfolio makes sense? A modest allocation limits the damage from moves like this one.
  • What is your time horizon? If you may need the money soon, volatility hits harder.
  • What does it cost? Physical gold carries a gap between buying and selling prices, and sometimes storage fees; ETFs and CFDs have fees and other charges.

Buying in stages, on several dates, avoids putting everything in at a single price, but it does not remove the risk of loss.

What are the risks of investing in gold?

  • Volatility: as this week showed, the price can drop several percent in just a few days.
  • No income: gold pays no interest or dividends, so returns depend entirely on price changes.
  • Currency risk: for euro investors, movements in the dollar affect the result.
  • Leverage: with CFDs and similar products, losses can quickly exceed the amount committed to the position.
  • Costs: spreads, fees and storage charges eat into returns.

What to watch in the coming weeks

The next moves will largely depend on three things: the December decisions of the US Federal Reserve and the ECB, the direction of the dollar, and how stock markets behave, particularly technology shares. Physical demand in India and China, where Reuters noted some pick-up in buying at lower prices, could also play a part.

Nobody knows for sure whether gold will keep falling or recover. The sensible approach is to look at your own situation, how much of your portfolio is in gold, and how much risk you can genuinely tolerate.

Frequently asked questions

What is the gold price right now?

On Friday 20 November 2026, gold traded as low as about $3,514 an ounce, its lowest level since September 2025, according to Reuters. Prices move throughout the day, so always check a live quote.

Why is gold going down?

Mainly because markets expect more interest rate rises from the US Federal Reserve, the dollar has strengthened, and some investors sold gold to cover losses after a sell-off in AI-related stocks.

How far has gold fallen from its record?

Gold hit its all-time high in late January 2026, above $5,500 an ounce. At this week's levels it is roughly a third below that peak.

Is now a good time to buy gold?

There is no guarantee of a rebound. A price below the record does not make gold cheap. Any decision should reflect your goal, time horizon, costs and how much of your portfolio gold represents.

Is the fall the same in euros as in dollars?

Not exactly. Because the dollar has risen, the drop measured in euros tends to be somewhat smaller. You are still exposed to both the gold price and the exchange rate.
